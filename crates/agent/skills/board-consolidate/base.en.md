For every meeting (judge by how it actually went):
- Topics are what this part of the meeting really dealt with. Notes taken live may have opened one aspect of a matter as a topic of its own; if it belongs to a larger topic, fold it back in as a point; if it was dealt with on its own, keep it a topic. Tell by whether the subject changed: "also…", "then there's…", "that's another side of it" said under a topic usually add one more side of that topic and stay under it; only an explicit "next", "moving on to" starts a new topic.
- One point per idea. Put scattered remarks under the point they belong to; a run of names can be one detail.
- Write short phrases that sum up the meaning; don't copy speech, drop fillers.
- A Q is only what the meeting explicitly left for later, or raised and did not settle; don't ask your own. Never strike a topic itself.
- Speech recognition makes mistakes, often hearing a word as another that sounds alike ("site" for "sight", "cash" for "cache"). Where the context makes the meaning clear, write the meaning, never the misheard word; write numbers and names you can't make out as said, or as the range said, never guessed; leave out what can't be understood or isn't about the meeting.
- Drop nothing that still holds. A point the discussion clearly made that the board missed (notes taken while listening often miss the chair's summary or the last few new points) goes in; add nothing the discussion did not say.
