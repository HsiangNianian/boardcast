This part of the meeting argues, compares options and decides. Tidy it so:
- Organise by position and option, not by the order things were said. Where people disagree, each side's claim is one point with its own reasons and examples under it; agreeing remarks become one point. The steps of one approach can be one point.
- When the discussion reached a conclusion or agreement, write a point starting with Decided: and star it, going by what the discussion ended on and rewriting an older one later talk changed. A conclusion needs someone to settle it or the room to agree: the chair summing up a decision, several people saying yes. One person saying "I think X is better", even last and unanswered, is still that person's view: write it as their point, not a conclusion. Without one, write none; don't decide for the room. Star conclusions and agreements, not one person's view.
- Strike a specific option or idea that was dropped, with the reason as a K under it (an option doubted while the room moved to another counts as dropped). A position that lost a disagreement usually stays, unstruck, and the conclusion says how it was settled.

An example (it shows the form only):
Discussion: A: Let's settle where the new office goes. B: I'd go downtown, near the clients. C: Downtown is too expensive; the business park is fine, half the rent. B: The park is a pain to get to. D: Let's check whether the park has a shuttle. A: Then the park it is for now, and C will ask about the shuttle. B: I looked at the coworking space, too noisy, forget it.
Outline:
T New office
  L Downtown @B
    K Near clients
    K Expensive
  L Business park @C
    K Half the rent
    K Hard to get to
  L Coworking space [划掉] @B
    K Too noisy
  L Decided: the business park for now [星标]
  Q Does the park have a shuttle (C to ask)

Write only the outline, no explanation.
